# Wiiter ♻️

**Before you throw it away, find its next use.**

*A civic guide for Basel residents to repair, give away, reuse, resell, or recycle everyday objects.*

## Overview

Wiiter is a civic-service prototype exploring how interaction design can make circular-economy options easier to find and act on in Basel, Switzerland.

Basel already has repair cafés, second-hand shops (Brockis), lending libraries, resale platforms, and collection points. The difficulty is knowing **which option fits a particular item**—and what to do next. Wiiter brings these possibilities together through a guided, understandable experience.

The goal is to make **keeping objects in use** easier than throwing them away.

## The problem

Information about repair, donation, reuse, and recycling is fragmented across providers, each with different conditions and acceptance rules. When finding a suitable option takes too much effort, disposal becomes the default.

Wiiter approaches this as an information-design and decision-support challenge: help people go from *“I have this item”* to *“Here is the best next action, and why.”*

## How it works

The proposed experience has three steps and requires no account:

1. **Describe it** — choose the item type and condition, optionally adding a photo.
2. **Find your option** — review a recommended next step, its reasoning, and relevant places.
3. **Give it a second chance** — repair, give away, reuse, resell, or recycle the object, then log the outcome.

The decision hierarchy emphasizes retaining value before recovering material:

**Repair → Give away → Reuse / Resell → Recycle**

## Prototype concept and features

### Guided item decisions
Help residents choose an appropriate second-life route based on an item's category and condition, with an explanation of the recommendation.

### Basel circular-economy directory
A map-oriented directory for finding relevant local services. The pitch identifies sources including repair cafés, Brockis, reuse and borrowing organisations, resale platforms, and recycling collection points.

The proposed directory can be filtered by action and item type, including electronics, furniture, clothing, bicycles, books, toys, and appliances.

### Personal impact dashboard
A way to record what happened to each item and display estimated outcomes such as:

- Items kept in use
- Waste avoided
- Estimated CO₂-equivalent avoided
- Value recovered

**Impact figures are illustrative estimates, not measured environmental outcomes.**

### Trust and accessibility
Wiiter is designed around several principles:

- **Reasons, not just answers:** explain why an option fits.
- **Labelled sources:** distinguish official information from illustrative entries.
- **Privacy by default:** no account required; outcomes are intended to stay on the resident's device.
- **Bilingual access:** German and English for Basel's diverse community.

These describe the design approach presented in the project pitch; individual implementation details should be validated in the source prototype.

## Future AI-assisted features

The following features are **planned concepts**, not claimed as implemented:

1. **Acceptance compatibility:** compare item descriptions or photographs with a provider's acceptance rules before a visit.
2. **Second-life optimizer:** consider repairability, resale value, and environmental trade-offs when suggesting a route.
3. **Route-to-action matching:** turn a suggestion into an actionable next step, such as a suitable location, instructions on what to bring, or a resale-listing draft.

## Design and research focus

Wiiter explores how **UX design, information architecture, and explainable decision support** can reduce the gap between environmental intentions and practical action.

Key questions include:

- How can fragmented civic resources become a clear decision pathway?
- How can recommendations communicate both their reasoning and limitations?
- How can estimated environmental impact be shown without implying false precision?
- How can a local public-interest service remain approachable without requiring registration?

## Project status

**Prototype / concept development.**

The project pitch proposes a path from prototype to public pilot:

1. **Verify:** confirm service hours and acceptance rules with listed providers.
2. **Test:** conduct usability tests with residents in a Basel neighbourhood.
3. **Pilot:** evaluate whether residents complete and log second-life actions.
4. **Grow:** allow community members and providers to suggest additional places.

These stages are a roadmap, not completed pilot results or confirmed partnerships.
